Data collection capabilities

Three ways to capture manipulation data.

01

Human Manipulation Demonstrations

Egocentric and multi-view recordings of human manipulation tasks, with structured metadata, task-level annotations, and environmental diversity.

02

Workplace & Real‑World Data Collection

Exploring partnerships with warehouses, manufacturing facilities, laundries, workshops, and other working environments to capture authentic human manipulation workflows with minimal disruption to existing operations.

03

Robot‑Compatible Demonstrations

Task-specific collection approaches using UMI-style interfaces, teleoperation, or other compatible recording systems, selected according to the customer's robot, task, and data requirements.

Chosen for technical compatibility with your robot — not interchangeable datasets.

Human video, robot-compatible demonstrations and robot action data are not the same thing.

Human video

Rich task context. Actions have to be inferred.

Robot-compatible demonstrations

Captured through an interface that matches the robot’s gripper.

Robot action data

The robot’s own commands — tied to one embodiment.

From task to dataset

How a data pilot comes together.

  1. 01

    Define Requirements

    Understand the target task, robot embodiment, observation/action modalities, environment, and acceptance criteria.

  2. 02

    Design the Protocol

    Select recording equipment, collection environments, operational workflows, permissions, and quality-control requirements.

  3. 03

    Capture & Validate

    Organize the agreed recording campaign and perform relevant video, synchronization, metadata, and trajectory quality checks.

  4. 04

    Prepare & Deliver

    Package the data in the customer-approved schema and provide appropriate documentation and validation reports.

Data quality & compatibility

Built around your learning pipeline.

Different robots require different data. Collection protocols must account for camera placement, timing, calibration, action representations, hardware compatibility, and task-specific success criteria.

Possible deliverables, depending on the project

  • 01Synchronized RGB video
  • 02Structured manipulation trajectories
  • 03Task annotations and episode metadata
  • 04Camera calibration information
  • 05Dataset quality reports
  • 06LeRobot-compatible or other customer-specified packaging
A file format is a container, not compatibility.

Repackaging human video as a LeRobot dataset doesn’t make it usable by a given robot. Compatibility is designed into the capture protocol from the start.
