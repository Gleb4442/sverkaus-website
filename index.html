How a Pilot Works

Start Small, Validate Before Scaling

Four concrete steps from initial task description to delivered pilot episodes.

01

Share the task and data requirements.

Outline the manipulation workflow, target modalities, environment constraints, and rough sample count.

02

Confirm participant access, equipment, and feasibility.

We verify skilled operator availability, workspace permissions, and whether camera or sensor hardware can be sourced or shipped.

03

Agree on pilot scope, price, and acceptance criteria.

Lock in recording protocols, timestamping/schema expectations, pass/fail QA checks, and milestone pricing.

04

Collect, validate, and deliver the agreed data.

Coordinate capture sessions, review episodes against the agreed acceptance criteria, and transfer the packaged dataset.

Throughput, hardware compatibility, and deliverable thresholds are scoped individually for each pilot rather than promised as universal guarantees.

Technical Requirements

Parameters Defined Together Before Recording

Technical specifications are defined jointly with your engineering team for each pilot. We do not advertise external pipeline standards as pre-supported unless verified against your ingestion stack.

Task definition and environment
Start/end episode boundaries, tool and workpiece state, lighting conditions, workspace geometry, and failure/recovery rules.
Sensor modalities and camera setup
Egocentric and/or fixed-angle RGB views, plus project-scoped stereo, depth, pose tracking, IMU, or force/tactile sensors where hardware is available.
Resolution, frame rate, and timestamps
Target pixel dimensions, exposure/shutter behavior for fast hand motion, frame cadence, and monotonic per-frame timestamp logging.
Calibration and synchronization
Intrinsics, multi-view extrinsics, coordinate reference frames, and hardware-trigger or software clock synchronization tolerances.
Data schema and delivery format
Directory layout, container/codec selection, sensor stream serialization, annotation format, and transfer mechanism matched to your pipeline.
Quality checks and acceptance criteria
Explicit rejection thresholds for occlusion, frame drops, sync drift, out-of-frame actions, or incomplete task executions before sign-off.
Hardware ownership and logistics
Whether recording uses baseline local camera setups, project-procured sensors, or customer-supplied rigs shipped to the collection location.
