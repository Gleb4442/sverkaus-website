Human video, robot-compatible demonstrations, and robot action data are not the same thing.

We scope every project around what each kind of data can and cannot give your model.

Dimension Human manipulation videoegocentric / multi-view Robot-compatible demonstrationsUMI-style handheld interfaces Hardware-specific action datateleoperation on the target robot
Observations RGB video of human hands and task context, from one or more viewpoints. Gripper-mounted and scene cameras, with placement chosen to match the target setup. The robot's own cameras and proprioception.
Actions Not recorded directly. Hand pose and motion must be estimated after capture. End-effector trajectories and gripper state, tracked from the interface. Commands in the robot's own action space (joint or end-effector).
Embodiment gap Large — a human hand is not a robot gripper. Reduced when gripper geometry and camera placement match the robot. None for that robot — but the data is bound to it.
Typical use Representation learning, task understanding, priors and pre-training signals. Policy learning for robots with a matching end-effector. Policy training and fine-tuning on that specific platform.

Environment choice follows the data you need: object categories, lighting, clutter, operator variation and the deployment conditions you are targeting.

Data quality & compatibility

Built around your learning pipeline.

Different robots require different data. Collection protocols must account for camera placement, timing, calibration, action representations, hardware compatibility, and task-specific success criteria.

Potential deliverables, depending on the project

  • D1Synchronized RGB video
  • D2Structured manipulation trajectories
  • D3Task annotations and episode metadata
  • D4Camera calibration information
  • D5Dataset quality reports
  • D6LeRobot-compatible or other customer-specified packaging
A file format is a container, not compatibility.

Converting arbitrary human video into a LeRobot or any other layout does not make it usable by a given robot. Viewpoint, timing, calibration and action representation have to be designed into the capture protocol from the start.
