Direct Summary

Sverkaus is an operational and engineering integrator for specification-driven Physical AI data production—not a stock marketplace of generic crowdsourced video. We never build speculative "shelf" datasets. Instead, we start from a robotics buyer's exact failure mode and sensor contract, clear active commercial workplaces via Site Security Clearance (GO / HOLD / NO GO), validate capture with a 10–20 minute shakedown, run on-site Edge QA (RELEASE / HOLD / REJECT), and account for Cost per Accepted Hour with recorded, submitted, and accepted hours tracked separately.

01 · Research-Grounded Transfer Principles

Why Lab Benches Plateau & Live Shifts Transfer.

Robotics models trained solely in simulation or on sterile studio loops routinely fail in unstructured commercial environments. Sverkaus structures every capture protocol around five empirically documented requirements of modern manipulation models (VLA, imitation learning, RL, and world models):

Principle 01 · Data Scaling Laws

Multi-Site Breadth Over Studio Repeats

As shown in Data Scaling Laws in Imitation Learning (arXiv:2410.18647), policy performance plateaus after roughly ~50 demonstrations per scene-object pair, while generalization scales as a power law with environmental and object diversity. Sverkaus sources real commercial shifts so lighting, clutter, wear, and surface friction vary naturally.

Principle 02 · ~2× Human-to-Robot Transfer

Synchronized Wrist Views + 3D Hand Kinematics

Plain head-mounted POV video suffers from grasp self-occlusion and viewpoint mismatch. Empirical transfer work (arXiv:2512.22414) demonstrates that pairing synchronized wrist-worn cameras with 3D hand tracking nearly doubles human-to-robot transfer on novel manipulation tasks.

Principle 03 · Contact & Force Signal

Tactile, Force & sEMG Where RGB Falls Short

RGB video alone cannot recover contact Newtons, slip onset, or compliant pressure on soft, fragile, or high-friction objects (FeelTheForce, arXiv:2506.01944). For contact-rich tasks, Sverkaus configures fingertip force/pressure sensors, MoCap gloves, or sEMG per buyer spec.

Principle 04 · Kinematic Compatibility (>3× Teleop)

Robot-Free UMI / Exoskeleton Capture + Reachability

Portable hand-held gripper and exoskeleton interfaces (DexUMI, arXiv:2505.21864) collect manipulation data >3× faster than robot teleoperation without hauling hardware onto a live shop floor—while Edge QA validates kinematic reachability for the client's target embodiment.

Principle 05 · Natural Failure & Recovery Quotas + Subgoals: Sanitized "perfect-only" clips fail when encountering fabric snags, cable tension, or slipped tools. Sverkaus preserves natural errors, corrective human recoveries, and step-level success/failure boundaries (never staging unsafe failures) alongside synced base+wrist frames for both policy learning and world-model subgoal conditioning.

05 · Technical FAQ

Common Engineering Questions.

Why do imitation learning and VLA robotics policies plateau on sterile lab-bench data?
Empirical scaling studies (arXiv:2410.18647) show that imitation policies typically plateau after roughly ~50 demonstrations in a single scene-object setup because additional repeats in the same lighting, background, and fixture geometry add almost no distributional diversity. Strong zero-shot generalization scales as a power law with breadth across distinct real environments and objects, where clutter, wear, lighting shifts, and friction occur naturally.
Why are synchronized wrist cameras, 3D hand kinematics, and tactile/sEMG sensors critical compared to plain head-cam video?
Plain head-mounted egocentric video suffers from self-occlusion at the point of contact and cannot recover contact force or finger torque. Research shows human-to-robot transfer nearly doubles when demonstrations include synchronized wrist-worn cameras and 3D hand kinematics (arXiv:2512.22414), while contact-rich tasks with soft, fragile, or high-friction materials require explicit tactile/force or sEMG streams (arXiv:2506.01944).
What does Edge QA check before a robotics data batch is released?
Sverkaus Edge QA validates frame drops, multi-stream timestamp synchronization drift, camera intrinsic and extrinsic calibration integrity, hand/tool visibility and occlusion scores, motion-blur and lighting thresholds, episode completeness, privacy Scoped FOV compliance, and kinematic reachability against the buyer's target robot morphology. Every segment is classified as RELEASE, HOLD, or REJECT before delivery, and recorded, submitted, and accepted hours are tracked separately.
Why does Sverkaus preserve real slips, failed grasps, and human recoveries instead of filtering for perfect demos?
Policies trained exclusively on clean, error-free demonstrations fail when encountering real-world friction, fabric snagging, or misaligned parts because they have never observed recovery states. Preserving natural slips, corrective human interventions, and step-level success/failure outcomes (without ever staging unsafe errors) provides the exact failure-mode signal needed for reinforcement learning (RL) and robust policy recovery.
